Psychological & neurodevelopmental assessments

Understanding strengths, difficulties and needs

A specialist assessment can help to explain how a person thinks, learns, pays attention and relates to others, and what support would make a difference at home, at school or at work. I assess children aged 11 and above, adolescents and adults, drawing on many years of diagnostic work in NHS and private clinics.

Each assessment combines clinical interviews, a developmental history and standardised measures with, where appropriate, information from family, school or other professionals. I take account of each person's emotional, educational and social circumstances, and of any difficulties that may occur alongside one another.

ADHD assessmentsAttention, concentration, organisation and impulsivity

ADHD can affect attention, organisation, impulse control and the regulation of emotions, and it does not always look the way people expect. Some young people are visibly restless; others are quietly inattentive, forgetful or exhausted by the effort of keeping up, and their difficulties may go unnoticed for years.

An assessment may help if there are persistent difficulties with:

  • Concentration, distractibility or forgetfulness
  • Organisation, planning and finishing tasks
  • Restlessness, impulsivity or frustration
  • Schoolwork that does not reflect apparent ability
  • Everyday routines and responsibilities

The assessment includes:

  • A developmental history with parents or carers
  • A clinical interview with the young person
  • Standardised questionnaires, including the Conners-4, completed at home, at school and by the young person
  • Careful consideration of other explanations and co-occurring conditions, with reference to DSM-5 criteria

Assessments for adults are available on enquiry.

Autism Spectrum Condition (ASC) assessmentsCommunication, relationships and sensory experience

Autism influences how a person communicates, relates to others and experiences sensory information. It can be less apparent in people who have learnt to adapt or to mask their differences, and some are not recognised until adolescence or adulthood. My approach is collaborative and neurodiversity-affirming.

An assessment may help where there are differences in:

  • Social communication and friendships
  • Reading unspoken social expectations
  • Coping with change, transitions or the unexpected
  • Sensitivity to sound, light, touch or other sensations
  • Focused interests and a preference for routine
  • Tiredness after social contact, or the strain of masking

The assessment includes:

  • A detailed developmental history
  • The ADOS-2, a structured observation adapted to age and language
  • Information from parents, school or others who know the person well, where appropriate
  • Careful consideration of other explanations and co-occurring conditions

Enquiries are welcome for children aged 11 and above, adolescents and adults.

Cognitive assessments (IQ testing)A detailed profile of how a person learns and processes information

A cognitive assessment looks beyond a single IQ score. Most people have areas of relative strength and difficulty, and a clear profile can explain why some tasks feel harder than others and how learning can be better supported. I use the WISC-V and other age-appropriate standardised measures.

An assessment may help when there are questions about:

  • Learning difficulties or underachievement
  • A gap between ability and school performance
  • Working memory or speed of processing
  • High ability or an uneven profile
  • Special educational needs and examination access arrangements
  • The effect of ADHD or autism on learning

The assessment looks at:

  • Verbal comprehension
  • Visual-spatial skills
  • Fluid reasoning
  • Working memory
  • Processing speed

A cognitive assessment can also be combined with an ADHD or autism assessment where this would be helpful.

Consultation and support for schoolsAdvice for staff on pupils' learning, attention and wellbeing

I work with schools to help staff make sense of a pupil's learning, attention, emotional and developmental needs, and to identify the adjustments that are most likely to help.

This may include:

  • Assessment of an individual pupil, with a report for the school and family
  • Consultation meetings with teachers or the SENCo
  • Practical classroom strategies and reasonable adjustments
  • Contributions to support planning and reviews

Arrangements are agreed with each school in advance.

What every assessment includes

Much of the work takes place outside the appointments themselves: reviewing information, scoring and interpreting results, and bringing the findings together. Every assessment includes:

  • A brief telephone discussion to clarify the questions the assessment should answer
  • A detailed written report setting out the findings, any diagnostic conclusions, strengths and practical recommendations for home, school, further education or work
  • A feedback session to talk through the findings and next steps
  • With your consent, liaison with schools or other professionals

EMDR – trauma-focused therapy

Eye Movement Desensitisation and Reprocessing

EMDR is an evidence-based psychological therapy that helps people to process and recover from traumatic or distressing experiences. When difficult experiences have not been fully processed, they can continue to affect emotions, thoughts, behaviour and physical wellbeing, sometimes long after the event itself.

EMDR helps the brain to process these memories in a way that reduces their emotional intensity, allowing you to move forward without feeling overwhelmed by the past. I offer EMDR to adults, adolescents and children aged 11 and above.

What to expect

Therapy begins with an assessment and preparation phase to ensure that EMDR is appropriate for your needs. The number of sessions depends on the nature of the difficulties being addressed, and the work always proceeds at a pace that feels safe for you.

I have extensive experience of working with PTSD, complex trauma and trauma-related difficulties, including those arising from:

  • Accidents and life-threatening experiences
  • Physical or sexual assault, abuse and domestic violence
  • Childhood trauma, neglect and adverse experiences
  • Bereavement and traumatic loss
  • Medical trauma, difficult childbirth and birth-related experiences
  • Witnessing traumatic events or experiencing secondary trauma
  • Terrorism, violence and other critical incidents

Where my experience comes from

Much of my specialist interest in trauma grew out of work abroad. In Madrid, I supported children, adults and bereaved families affected by the terrorist attacks of 11 March 2004, helping them through grief and the aftermath of a traumatic event. In Kigali, Rwanda, with the Service de Consultations Psychosociales, I worked with survivors of the 1994 Genocide against the Tutsi and with people affected by sexual violence. That experience of complex trauma, profound loss and severe distress has shaped how I work ever since.

In London, much of my work has been with children, young people and families. At Kids Company I supported highly vulnerable young people facing neglect, abuse, domestic violence, bereavement and attachment difficulties. At Children's Therapies, a private multidisciplinary clinic, I offered assessment and therapy for anxiety, low mood, attachment difficulties and post-traumatic stress. At Newham CAMHS' Children's Development Centre (East London NHS Foundation Trust), I carried out autism assessments for children aged 2 to 18, alongside wider CAMHS work.

I have also served as a member of the French Embassy's Psychological Response Team, supporting people affected by crises and emergencies, and worked as an associate psychologist with PsychWorks, providing consultancy and medico-legal work around trauma, adjustment to illness and disability, and family relationships.
